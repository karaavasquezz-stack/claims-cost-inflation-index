About this forecast

Both models are fit on the same monthly average property settlement cost series (buildings + contents combined). Prophet uses the change in the rebased HICP index and the construction wage index as exogenous regressors, decomposing the series into trend, yearly seasonality, and inflation-driven effects. SARIMAX uses a grid-searched seasonal specification with month-on-month HICP change as its exogenous regressor. Future regressor values are projected forward from their recent trailing trend. The central scenario (default) applies no additional adjustment beyond the models' own baseline.

Seasonality — monthly effect on claim cost (Prophet vs SARIMAX)

Prophet's yearly seasonal component is extracted from a synthetic year with regressors held at their historical mean. SARIMAX seasonality is derived from the average within-month deviation in the model's in-sample fitted values. Both are expressed as a % effect above or below the annual average — positive means that month tends to run higher.

Historical backtest — trained on 2020–2024, forecasting 2025

Both models were refit using only data up to the end of 2024, then asked to forecast every month of 2025. Comparing those forecasts to what actually happened gives an honest, out-of-sample error margin for each model — this is a stronger accuracy test than fitting and reporting error on the same data.

Model guide — key terms explained
Prophet
A forecasting model developed by Meta that splits a time series into trend, seasonality, and the effect of external regressors (here, HICP and construction wage changes). It's robust to missing data and easy to interpret component-by-component.
SARIMAX
Seasonal AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average with eXogenous regressors. Generalises classic ARIMA to handle yearly seasonal cycles and external inputs (here, the month-on-month change in HICP) at the same time.
Scenario presets
Low inflation: models a soft macroeconomic environment — ECB tightening, subdued wage growth, no additional severity uplift. Good for best-case reserving. Central: the baseline forecast using current CSO inflation trends with moderate severity uplift (5%/yr). High pressure: persistent inflation, strong construction wage growth, elevated legal costs, and a seasonal uplift — appropriate for stress-testing reserves.
90% confidence interval (CI)
The shaded band around the Prophet forecast. There's a 90% chance the actual future value falls within this range, based on the model's estimated uncertainty.
MAPE
Mean Absolute Percentage Error — the average % difference between a model's forecast and the actual value. Lower is better; under 15% is considered good for claims cost data.
Backtest
Training a model only on past data (here, 2020–2024) and testing it against a later period it never saw (2025), to get an honest read on real-world accuracy.
Seasonality
The recurring within-year pattern in claim costs — e.g. certain months consistently running higher or lower than the underlying trend, independent of overall inflation. The seasonality tab now shows both Prophet's decomposed seasonal component and SARIMAX's data-driven monthly pattern for comparison.
YoY (Year-on-Year) %
The percentage change in average claim cost compared to the same month one year earlier. Highlights momentum that a smoothed trend line can hide.
Construction wage proxy
Average weekly earnings in the construction sector, used as a rough proxy for the labour-cost component of property reinstatement (rebuild and repair) claims.
CPI
Consumer Price Index — Ireland's official measure of general price inflation across a basket of household goods and services.
HICP
Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices — the EU-standardised inflation measure, allowing direct comparison of Ireland's inflation with other EU member states.
Pearson correlation (r)
A statistic from -1 to +1 measuring how closely two series move together. +1 means they move in perfect lockstep, 0 means no relationship, -1 means they move in perfect opposition.
